# 00 — Game Vision

## One sentence
Ashes of Dominion is a turn-based tactical strategy RPG deckbuilder where you command a persistent army of stacks, personally order their attacks, and turn a battle with unit skills and Hero powers.

## Core fantasy
> "I built this army, positioned my troops, personally ordered their attacks, and at the critical moment used my units' special abilities and my Hero's powers to turn the battle."

The game must **not** feel like a card game where cards merely stand in for attacks. Units attack for free; cards *augment* what units do.

## Design pillars
1. **Command, don't cast.** Basic attacks are first-class, manual and free. Cards change the outcome, not replace the action.
2. **Numbers are bodies.** A stack's count is its health (Heroes 3 model). Losing HP means losing soldiers, and soldiers stay lost between battles.
3. **Position matters.** 6v6 lanes; melee is blocked by the front row, only ranged reaches the back.
4. **Attrition creates decisions.** Go deeper with a hurt army, or return to town at a cost. (Town return/Threat is planned, not built yet.)
5. **Readable at a glance.** The player can always answer: which unit can act, what can it hit, what will happen, what will I lose.
6. **Small, sharp roster.** 3 Heroes, 4 friendly units, 4 enemy units, one boss. Depth from combinations, not volume.

## Influences
Disciples: Sacred Lands (6v6 positional combat), Heroes of Might and Magic III (stacks, casualties, town), Slay the Spire (cards, rewards, shop, relics), D&D-style Hero stats, Diablo (hub + dungeon), Dark Souls / Darkest Dungeon (retreat has a cost).

## Anti-goals (MVP)
No AP/DP, no Energy, no automatic player attacks, no initiative/speed, no reactions/interrupts, no 20-status library, no multiple towns, no city-builder sprawl, no meta-progression, no multiplayer.

## Tone and feel
Dark-fantasy, grounded, a little grim. Interface is ornate and warm (wood, iron, gold, red) rather than clean-modern. Premium indie polish, not AAA: consistent, legible, no visual noise.

## Success test
A new player understands a turn without a tutorial; a veteran keeps finding reasons to change their army and deck between fights.
